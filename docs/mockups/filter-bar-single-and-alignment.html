The filter bar: a lone filter, and a floating checkbox

Three things on Items & inventory, all in the same 147px of card. Measured from the running app at 1440.

1 & 3 — the checkbox floats, and that is also the "unequal padding"measured

The panel is a grid of equal columns and its cells stretch. A select brings a label with it, so its control sits at the bottom of the cell; the checkbox has no label above it, so it sits at the top. Measured: the select's centre is at y=327 and the checkbox's at y=292 — 35px apart. The row reads as ragged rather than as a row, and the space under the checkbox reads as padding that is missing from above it.

Fixedrecommended

The checkbox is aligned to the control beside it, not to the top of the cell: the cell becomes a flex column with the checkbox pushed to the bottom, so it lands on the select's own line. That is what Ant Design, Carbon and Polaris all do with a lone checkbox in a filter row, and it is what the app's own essentials_field_row does with a short field.

On four pages you click a disclosure to reveal one control, and on two of those the control is a single checkbox. design.md currently says every bar collapses whatever its size, and the reason recorded is a good one: "two behaviours across sixteen pages is worse than one — learning the filter bar on donations and then meeting something else on transfers teaches nothing transferable."

Option A — leave itstatus quo

One behaviour, and one click to reach one checkbox on /vendors. The consistency argument is real; the cost is that the least filtered pages are the ones that ask for the most work.

Option B — a lone filter is shown, never hiddenrecommended

When the bar holds exactly one control, there is nothing to disclose: the control is the bar. Two or more keep the button.

/vendors — one checkbox, no button.
Status
/partners — one select, no button.
  • It is still one rule, which is what the recorded objection was really about: the bar shows its filters; when there are enough to be worth folding away, it folds them. Nothing is hidden that you have to learn to look for — the opposite.
  • Nothing is lost. The chips and "Clear all" only ever appear when something is applied, and they still do.
  • Four pages get shorter and lose a click each.
  • Cost: a reader moving from /vendors to /donations meets a button they did not see before. That is the objection — but a filter that is *visible* teaches nothing wrong, and a page with nine of them plainly needs the fold.

Option C — the first row inline, "More filters" for the restbigger change

Polaris, Stripe, Jira and GitHub all do this: the common filters are always on screen and the overflow is behind a button.

The most standard answer, and the largest one: it needs a decision about which filters are the common ones on every page with more than two, and a responsive rule for what drops at narrow widths. Worth doing on its own, not as part of fixing an alignment.

Recommendation

Fix the alignment, and take Option B. The checkbox joins the select's line, which also removes the "missing padding". And a bar with a single control shows it, because a disclosure in front of one checkbox is a click that buys nothing — while a page with nine filters keeps the fold that earns its place.
