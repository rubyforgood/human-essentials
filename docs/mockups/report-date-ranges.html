Report date ranges, parameters and chart styles

Three questions: what the industry does about date ranges on a chart, whether these pages want month/year granularity, and what other controls or chart shapes would earn their place. Every chart below is drawn from this bank's real figures.

1. Where the app stands

PageDate control
Itemized donations / distributions / requests, Activity graph, Manufacturer donations, By county Yes — 11 presets plus a custom From/To, day granularity
Monthly distributions / donations / purchases None. Hard-coded to the last twelve months, inside the service

The charts are the only reports you cannot re-aim, and the window is welded into HistoricalTrendService: where(issued_at: 1.year.ago.beginning_of_month..Time.current) with twelve fixed buckets.

2. The catch with the picker we already have

It is day-granular. On a chart that buckets by month that produces partial months at both ends — a first and last column that are short for a reason the reader cannot see and the axis does not state.

“12 Mar – 20 Aug” on a monthly chart. The end columns cover 20 days each; the ones between cover 30 or 31.

The two shortest columns are the two you cannot compare. Nothing says they are partial, so a reader sees a fall at the end that is an artefact of the range.

3. What the industry does

SystemApproachPartial periods
Google Analytics 4Presets + custom, day granularity, with an explicit Apply Marks the current, incomplete period and warns on it
GrafanaTimeRangePicker plus a separate interval control The bucket is chosen independently of the window
MetabaseDate filter with an explicit granularity — day, week, month, quarter Snaps the range to whole buckets
Xero, QuickBooksMonth/year pickers on monthly reports A partial month cannot be expressed; the problem is designed out
StripePresets + custom; the chart re-buckets to suit the span Granularity follows the window automatically

The split is clean. Where a chart buckets by a period, the control is in that period, or the granularity is a control of its own. Nobody offers a day-granular range over a monthly chart and leaves the partial months unexplained.

4. Option A — month/year range recommended

The same popover as the existing picker, the same presets-plus-custom shape, the same apply-as-you-choose behaviour. Only the field type changes: <input type="month"> instead of type="date".

Months Sep 2025 – Aug 2026

Applied as you choose them.

Every column covers a whole month. Nothing to explain, because nothing is partial.
  • For: the control's granularity matches the chart's, so the partial-month problem cannot occur. type="month" is native, like the type="date" the app already uses — no widget, no dependency, and it keeps the “no calendar library” rule.
  • Against: a second kind of date control in one app. Mitigated by everything except the field type being identical to the existing one.

5. Option B — window presets only

One click, nothing to get wrong, and it fits on a phone.
  • For: the smallest thing that answers “show me more, or less”. Every preset is a whole number of months by construction.
  • Against: cannot answer “what did last spring look like”, and for a bank comparing one grant year with another that is the question.

8. Other chart styles, on the same figures

Line instead of columns for long windows

At 24 or 36 months columns get thin and a line does not. Worth switching to automatically once the window passes about 18 points.

Stacked by item category recommended, with the filter

UncategorisedCategory TwoCategory OneCategory Three
Four bands, not forty-seven. This is the composition view that “top 8 and Other” was rejected for — because a category is a grouping the bank already maintains, not an arbitrary cut.

Calendar heatmap

Compact, and good for seasonality across several years. At twelve months it says less than the columns and reads less precisely.
Recommendation: A, plus compare, plus category. A for the range — a month/year picker in the same popover as the existing one, so the partial-month problem is designed out rather than explained away. Compare with the previous period, as a faded line, because it is the question every trend prompts and the one this page cannot answer. A category filter, with the stacked view when none is chosen — four bands from a grouping the bank already maintains, which is the honest composition answer that a top-N cut could not give.

Leaving out: granularity (no window long enough to need it yet), partner and location filters (answered better elsewhere), cumulative (no target to run against), heatmap (says less than the columns at this span).
