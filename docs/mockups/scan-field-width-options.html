How wide should the barcode field be?

Its left edge already aligns with everything — the scan bar and the cards above it all sit on the card's px-5, so they start on the same vertical at every width. It is the right edge that is unmoored. Every number below was measured on /donations/new.

The 1024 row is the real fault.

A fixed 240px against a fluid three-column grid does not merely stop short of a line — at 1024px it is 33px wider than the column it sits in, so it crosses a boundary that every other control on the page respects. A fixed width cannot align to a fluid grid at more than one viewport, and 240px happens to align at none of them.

The two schools, and which one this is

Fill the grid cell Carbon, Material, Polaris, Fluent

The layout grid is the discipline. A control takes the cell it is given; its right edge therefore lands on a line by construction, and nothing has to be measured per field.

Size to the content GOV.UK, USWDS

Short values of known length get a deliberately narrow field — govuk-input--width-10 and friends — on the reasoning that width tells you how much is wanted. A ragged right edge is accepted as the cost.

Both are right, about different situations, and I applied the wrong one.

GOV.UK's narrow fields sit in a column of stacked fields. The field above and the field below share the left edge, and that repetition is what carries the rhythm — the ragged right reads as deliberate because there is an obvious column holding everything. This field has no neighbours. It is one control alone in a tinted band, so there is no rhythm for a short width to sit inside, and it reads as unfinished. That is what you are seeing.

Option B — Half the card

540px at 1440. Aligns with a half, which is a line the page does not otherwise draw — the cards use thirds at lg, so a half falls between column two and column three. It comes close at md, where the grid is two columns — but measured, it overshoots by 10px, which is exactly half the gap-x-5 gutter: a grid column is (W − 20) / 2 and a half is W / 2. So it is near-miss at one breakpoint and plainly arbitrary at the rest. Also 540px of box for a 14-digit barcode.

240px at every width, which is the GOV.UK answer applied where its precondition does not hold. Defensible if the scan bar ever gains a second field beside it — a storage location, say — because then there would be a column of stacked controls for a short width to sit inside.

Recommendation

Option A. Put the scan bar on the same grid as the two cards above it and give it column one. Your instinct to match Storage location is the right one, and it is the stronger of the two you offered: a half is a line this page does not draw at lg, whereas a column is a line every other control on the page already sits on.

It also fixes the 1024 defect for free. Fluid columns cannot overshoot; a fixed width against a fluid grid was always going to be wrong at every viewport but one.

What this costs. 346px of field for 138px of content at the widest, which is the thing content-sizing is meant to prevent. I think alignment wins here because the field is alone in its band with nothing to establish a rhythm — but it is a real trade, not a free win, and design.md currently states the content-sizing rule without this caveat. If A is chosen I will correct that entry rather than leave the two contradicting each other.
