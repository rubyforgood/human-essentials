2. Why a better palette cannot fix it

I tried. A single-hue lightness ramp is the usual answer, and it still fails: indigo 900/700/500/300 gives adjacent ratios of 1.43, 1.76 and 2.16. So I worked out the ceiling instead of guessing at more palettes.

At most three fills can be mutually 3:1 apart on a white card — the luminance steps are 0.0, 0.10 and 0.40, and the next one is past white. Of those three, only two are also 3:1 against the card itself. Four bands distinguished by fill alone is arithmetically impossible, not a palette I have yet to find.

The three fills that are mutually 3:1 apart. The third is already too light to sit on a white card.

3. Four ways out

Option A — stop stacking; let the filter do it recommended

One series, one colour, exactly as the chart was approved. The Category filter beside the months answers “which category” one at a time, and the table below already holds every item. Nothing is distinguished by colour, so nothing can fail to be.

Zero colour dependence. Also the shortest, and the one that already works on a phone.

Option B — small multiples, one chart per category recommended, with A

Every category gets its own small chart in the same colour. Identity comes from position and a heading, not from a swatch — so there is no legend to reconcile and no pair of colours to compare. This is the standard answer to “compare a handful of series”.

  • For: answers “which category is driving the peak” better than a stack does, because the shapes sit side by side instead of on top of each other.
  • Against: the month-by-month total is no longer one shape — which is what option A's chart above it is for.

Option C — keep the stack, label the bands directly

Colour becomes decoration and the words carry the meaning. Works — but only where a band is thick enough to hold its name, and the thin ones are exactly the ones you cannot otherwise identify.

Option D — patterns as well as colour

Hatch, dots, cross, solid. Distinguishable without colour, and genuinely noisy at twelve columns — and it still needs a legend, which is the thing that was not working.

4. And the categories themselves

“Category One / Two / Three” are not real. They are seed data, from db/seeds.rb:130 — FactoryBot.create(:item_category, organization: org, name: "Category #{letter}"). Every organization in the development database has the same three placeholder names. A real bank names its own: Nappies, Period products, Adult incontinence.
QuestionAnswer
Who adds themAny signed-in bank user — org user or org admin, no separate permission. Verified: user_1@example.com gets HTTP 200 on /item_categories/new. Partners cannot.
WhereItems & inventory → the Categories tab, /item_categories
ScopePer organization. The name is unique within a bank, and two banks can use the same name for different things.
What else they doThey gate partner groups: ItemCategory has_and_belongs_to_many :partner_groups. All four partner groups here are tied to categories — it is how a bank says which items a group of agencies may request. So a category is not just a reporting label; renaming or deleting one touches what partners can ask for.

Recommendation: A with B. The main chart goes back to one series in one colour, and a small-multiples strip underneath gives each category its own shape at a glance. Between them there is no pair of colours anywhere that a reader has to tell apart, which is the only version of this that passes rather than nearly passes. C is the fallback if you want the stack kept; D I would not ship.
