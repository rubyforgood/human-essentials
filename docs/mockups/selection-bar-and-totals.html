The selection bar, and where the totals button goes

Two things reported on Requests: the bar that appears when you tick rows is "blue background and no padding", and "Show product totals pushes the table down by a lot". Both measured from the running app at 1440.

What is there nowmeasured

The bar is brand-50 with a 16px corner radius and no margin at its sides — so a rounded, tinted box sits hard against the card's edges with nothing between them. That is the "no padding": not the bar's own (it has 10×16), but the absence of any gap around it. Underneath, a 63px card header band exists to hold one 30px button.

Part one: the selection bar

Every option below replaces the card's header band rather than adding a row to it, which is Carbon's TableBatchActions and Material's contextual toolbar: the bar and the button it displaces occupy the same 63px, so the table does not move at all. Full bleed, no corner radius, a rule underneath like every other band on this card. Tick a checkbox in any of them.

Loud enough to notice against a white card, quiet enough not to be the only dark object in the app, and the indigo already means "this is the thing you are on" — a current tab, a focus ring, a selected row. What the industry does

SystemSelection bar
Carbon (IBM)TableBatchActions replaces the table toolbar in place, full bleed, inverse background

GitHub, GmailThe list header's controls swap; no colour change
Linear, Notion, DriveA floating dark pill near the bottom of the viewport

Three of the four keep it in the toolbar rather than inserting a new row, which is the part that matters here: an inserted row moves the table, and a replaced one does not. The floating pill is the odd one out and suits an app whose list fills the window; this one has a page header, tabs and filters above it already.

Recommendation

A1, with the totals button on the filter row. The bar takes the band the button vacates, so selecting a row moves nothing; the table starts 63px higher than it does today; and the bar is a full-bleed band with the card's own rule under it rather than a rounded box wedged against its edges.
