Choosing what the trend chart draws

You are right about the friction, and it measures worse than it reads. I also agree the category control should not be a second, separate thing. Below: what it costs today, your proposal drawn out, and three alternatives.

This was foreseeable and I did not foresee it. The checkbox submits a form, the form is a GET, and a GET is a new page. I checked that the cap worked and never checked what four selections cost end to end.

2. Option A — your proposal: one multi-select recommended

One control replaces both the filter-bar category select and the row checkboxes. It picks categories, narrows the table, and draws a line for each — so there is one answer to “what am I looking at”, not two. Selections apply when the panel closes: one request for four choices, and no scrolling at all.

Three chosen, three lines, each with its own dash pattern. The chips in the trigger say what is on the chart without opening anything.
  • For: no scrolling, one request, one control, and the chips double as the chart's key. It is what Amplitude and Mixpanel ship for exactly this.
  • Against: you can no longer compare two individual items — only categories. The 47 items stay in the table, unplottable.
  • On spacing: the two controls sit on one row, 27px apart today. Keeping them together is deliberate — both govern the chart and the table, so putting one above the table would say it only affects the table.

One searchable list, grouped. Keeps per-item comparison, which A gives up.
  • For: everything A does, plus the item-level question the original 47-line chart was clumsily trying to answer.
  • Against: a list of 62 mixed things, where a reader has to notice which group a name came from. Two kinds of thing in one control is the cost.

4. Option C — no plotting at all

The chart is always the total for the window and the chosen categories. The table answers “which”, with a sparkline on every row.
  • For: the least machinery of anything here. No cap, no chips, no dash patterns, nothing to explain. The sparkline column already gives every one of the 47 items its own shape.
  • Against: two items cannot be compared on one axis. Whether that matters is a question about how these reports are actually used, and I do not know the answer.

5. Option D — keep the checkboxes, fix the friction

The reported problem is the reload, not the checkbox. Applying into a turbo frame with the scroll kept would make ticking a row cost nothing and leave the row where it was.

  • For: smallest change; keeps per-item plotting; the table is where the items already are.
  • Against: it does not fix the real cost. Even with no reload you still scroll a 47-row table to find each thing you want. The dropdown removes the search; the frame only removes the flinch.
Recommendation: A, and it is your proposal. One multi-select, beside Months, replacing both the single-select and the row checkboxes; chips in the trigger doubling as the chart's key; applied on close so four choices cost one request. The cap of four stays — it was measured against dash patterns and label collisions, and none of that changes.

Take B instead if comparing two individual items matters; the price is a 62-entry list of two different kinds of thing. Take C if nobody actually compares series on this page, in which case all of this machinery should go and the sparklines carry it.

D is not enough on its own — and the scroll bug gets fixed regardless of which you pick, because losing 1,702px is a defect rather than a design choice.

One thing I would like to know before building, because it decides between A and C: does anyone compare two series on this page today, or is the question always “how much, and of what”? The 47-line chart that was here suggests someone once wanted comparison; the fact that every series arrived hidden suggests nobody used it.
