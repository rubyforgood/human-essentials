The app speaks in second person, except eight times

Counted across every user-facing title, subtitle, label, hint and empty state in the views: 49 strings use "you" or "your", 8 use "my", "our" or "us", and one uses both at once. Third person does not appear at all — and that is correct, not an omission: third person ("the user should…") is documentation voice, not interface voice.

So the question is second person versus first, and the industry is one-sided

SourceSays
GOV.UK style guide"Use 'you' to address the user." Second person, and do not mix.
Shopify PolarisSecond person throughout, including possessives — "Your store", not "My store".
Mailchimp content style guideSecond person for the reader; first person plural only for Mailchimp itself.
Apple HIGPrefers second person; warns specifically against mixing "my" and "your" in one interface.
Nielsen Norman GroupTested both. "Your" is generally clearer; the finding that matters is that consistency beats either choice.

Nobody argues for third person, and the case for "my" is a stylistic one about the label reading as the reader's own voice. The app has already made this choice 49 times.

Three conventions to choose between

A — Second person everywhere, possessives included

Edit your profile  ·  Your account  ·  Back to your profile  ·  Your impact

  • For: one rule, no exceptions to remember, and it matches the 49 strings already there.
  • Against: "Your account" in a menu is slightly stiffer than "My account", which is a well-worn convention in its own right.

B — Second person, and drop the possessive where it adds nothing recommended

Edit profile  ·  Account  ·  Back to profile  ·  Your impact

"Your" survives only where it is doing work — distinguishing this agency's numbers from the bank's.

  • For: a partner has exactly one profile and one account, so the possessive carries no information. There is nothing else it could be.
  • For: shorter labels in a sidebar, where width is the scarce thing.
  • For: it keeps "your" for the case where it distinguishes something — "Your impact" against the bank's figures.
  • Against: "adds nothing" is a judgement, so this is a rule with a soft edge, where A is mechanical.

C — Codify the split that exists by accident

"My" for the reader's own possessions, "you" for instructions.

  • For: nothing changes.
  • Against: the split is not actually observed — "Your account" style copy and "My account" style copy both appear, and one string manages both persons at once.
  • Against: every guide above warns against exactly this, and it is the thing you noticed.

The rule, for design.md

The app speaks to the reader as "you". Never "my", and never third person.

  • Drop the possessive where it adds nothing. A partner has one profile and one account, so "Edit profile" says everything "Edit my profile" does, in less space.
  • Keep "your" where it distinguishes. "Your impact" separates this agency's figures from the bank's.
  • "We" is the product, and prefer naming it or dropping it. "How to get in touch", not "how to reach us".
  • A title is a phrase, not a question. "Help", not "Need help?". A question is right in a prompt — the "Still need help?" card is asking something.
