The rail: four things wrong with it

All four reported at once, and they are not independent — three of them were introduced together by the last change, and they make each other worse.

“the scroll bar is very dark, when the user scrolls to the bottom of the page it hovers in an odd way. it also has no padding above the pagination, and doesnt have a border radius so it does not match with the rest of the components.”

Everything below is measured on /distributions at 1280×800 against the running app, not estimated.

Defect 1

The corners never rendered — and this one is just a bug

The CSS asks for a pill on both the track and the thumb:

.table-rail-track::beforeborder-radius: var(--radius-full)
.table-rail-thumbborder-radius: var(--radius-full)

--radius-full does not exist. It is referenced twice and defined nowhere — not in application.css, not in the compiled output. Tailwind v4 has no such token: its scale stops at --radius-2xl, and rounded-full compiles to calc(infinity * 1px) directly rather than through a variable. So the declaration is invalid, the browser drops it, and both parts render square. Measured computed border-radius on the live page: 0px on the track and 0px on the thumb.

At 3×, what ships against what was intended:

Now — square

Intended — pill

No option to choose here — it is a one-word fix. But it matters for defect 2: a hard-edged 600px × 6px bar reads considerably heavier than the same colour as a pill, so some of “very dark” is this.

Defect 2

Very dark — and there is room to lighten it

The thumb ships at slate-500, which measures 4.35:1 against its slate-100 track. WCAG 1.4.11 asks for 3:1, because a custom scrollbar thumb is author content rather than a platform control. So it is not that dark by requirement — it overshoots the floor by a wide margin, and the previous change went to slate-500 because it was the next step on the Tailwind scale, not because 4.35 was needed.

The scale has nothing between slate-400 and slate-500, and that gap is exactly where the answer sits. Solving for the lightest slate-hued value that still clears the floor:

Defect 3

Nothing between the bar and the pagination

The card reserves a strip for the rail to settle into — [data-railed] { padding-bottom: 24px } — and the rail is 24px tall. The strip is therefore exactly the height of the thing that goes in it, so the rail fills it edge to edge and its bottom lands 0.14px above the pagination’s top border. Two horizontal lines a hair apart, one of them a control.

The strip goes to 32px and the rail keeps its 24, leaving 8px of clear space above the border. 8 rather than 12 because the pagination adds its own 12px (py-3) below that border, so the eye reads 8 + rule + 12 as one gap and a larger number starts to look like a missing row.

Defect 4

“Hovers in an odd way”

The rail is position: fixed. While the table runs past the bottom of the window it rides the fold; once the table’s end comes on screen it settles into the reserved strip. Traced down /distributions, which scrolls 611px in total:

It floats for 448 of the 611 pixels — 73% of the scroll. For all of that it lies across live table rows. That was survivable when it had a 92%-white backdrop and a hairline above it, which is what separated it from the text underneath. The last change removed both, on the reasoning that with the native scrollbar hidden the rail no longer had to hold its own against a second bar. That reasoning was about the settled state, where nothing is behind it — and it took the backdrop away from the floating state too, where something always is.

One reading I could not test. “Hovers oddly” might instead mean it judders or trails the page during momentum scrolling. That is the classic symptom of a fixed element repositioned from a scroll handler, and I cannot reproduce it here: with synthetic scrolls the rail measured 0px from its correct position on every sampled frame. Option C fixes that reading whether or not I can see it, which is why it is in the recommendation as well.

A — leave the floating bar transparent

What ships today: a dark bar lying directly on the row text.

B — give it a ground, but only while it floats

Recommended

The backdrop and hairline come back, gated on a data-floating attribute the controller already knows how to set — it is the same test it uses to place the rail. When the rail settles into the reserved strip it drops both again, so the tidy version you chose is what you see at rest, and the row underneath stops being washed out the moment there is no row.

C — stop being a floating element once it settles

Recommendedwith B

Nothing visual — a mechanism change. Today the rail is fixed the whole time, so even at rest its position is recomputed by JavaScript on every scroll frame. Once the table’s end is on screen it does not need to be: it can be absolute inside the strip the card already reserves, and then the browser moves it with the page for free. At the bottom of the page — which is exactly where you are seeing the problem — there would be no per-frame repositioning left to go wrong. It stays fixed only while genuinely riding the fold.

D — never float at all

Costs the feature

The rail could simply live at the end of the table and never ride the fold, which removes the complaint completely. The cost is the reason the rail exists: on a long table the only horizontal scroll control would be below the fold again, and the platform will not supply one because its scrollbar is an overlay taking 0px. Worth having on the table, but it undoes rather than repairs.

What I would ship

1. Fix --radius-full → calc(infinity * 1px). A bug, not a choice.
2. Thumb to oklch(0.636 0.044 257.1) — option B, the lightest value that still clears 1.4.11 at 3.10:1.
3. Reserved strip 24px → 32px, for 8px of air above the pagination rule.
4. Backdrop back while floating only (B), and absolute once settled (C).

Together these are one coherent change: the rail is quiet and tidy where it rests, and legible where it has to sit on top of something.

Tell me which of the four you want and I will build exactly that. If “very dark” still describes option B when you see it in the app, say so — below 3:1 is available, it just has to be a decision taken with the number in front of us rather than by accident.
