B — Centre it

Ruled out by measurement

What you asked for, and the one number that decides it: every h1 in this app sits at 288 — index pages, show pages, form pages, all of them. Centring the form moves its heading, so the page title jumps sideways as you navigate:

  • Click "New item" from the items list and the title slides 208px right. The heading is the thing your eye returns to, and it would move between two views of the same resource.
  • It is also not the convention it is assumed to be. Centring is the pattern for standalone pages — sign in, checkout, onboarding — which have no surrounding furniture to align to. An in-app form under a page heading is left-aligned in GOV.UK, GitHub, Stripe, Shopify and Atlassian alike, sharing the heading's edge.
  • This app's own auth pages are not centred either: a split panel, form at left 888, width 384.

A — Widen the column, keep it left

New item
256px
  • For: one line changed per form, alignment preserved, dead space down from 480 to 256.
  • Against: it makes a text box for a 20-character name 896px wide. The space gets filled by stretching fields that had no reason to grow, and the eye travels further from label to value.

C — Use the width: short fields two-up

Recommended
New item
256px
  • The width earns itself. New item has ten fields and six are numeric — value, distribution quantity, minimum on hand, recommended on hand, package size. Paired two-up they fill the row without any single field growing past what it holds, and the form gets shorter as well as wider.
  • It is form-dependent, which is the honest answer. Kits has three fields and nothing to pair, so it stays as it is. Vendors has five, two of them short. A blanket width would have pretended every form is the same shape.
  • Against: more than one line per form, and it needs a judgement per form about which fields belong side by side. Getting that wrong pairs two things that are not related.
  • Against: a two-column form is harder to scan down for a screen reader user if the order is not right — the DOM order has to stay the order you would fill them in.

Recommendation

C, and write the rule down. Keep the left edge — it is the industry standard for a form under a page heading, and it is the only thing keeping every title in this app on the same line. Fill the space by pairing short fields on forms that have enough of them, and leave three-field forms alone.

A is the cheap version if you would rather not touch field layout: one line per form, half the dead space, at the cost of very wide inputs.

Either way design.md gains the rule it never had — a form is left-aligned at the page gutter, and here is the width to use.
