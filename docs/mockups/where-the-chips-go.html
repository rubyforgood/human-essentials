Where the chips go

You are right that they belong near the control. The measurements decide between the two, and they are not close.

1. The numbers

Measured on four chipsValue
The four chips, with gaps659px
The Compare field256px — one chip and a bit
The filter bar row1,120px — all four, with 461px to spare
One chip's height26px, so a row costs ~34px

Four chips cannot fit inside the field. At 256px it holds one and an overflow badge. On a row of its own it holds all four with room left, and costs a fixed 34px however many there are.

2. What the design systems do

Read from the source this morning, not recalled:

  • IBM Carbon's MultiSelect puts a selectionCount badge inside the field, with a clearSelection control beside it. No chips in the field.
  • Shopify Polaris ships Tag as a removable tag used in a row, separate from any select.
  • Material distinguishes an input chip, which lives inside a text field you type into — email recipients, tags you invent — from a filter chip, which lives in a chip set, a row of its own. This control is the second kind: the options already exist and you are choosing among them.
Two of the three put the summary in the field and the chips in a row. That is also the only arrangement the width allows here.

One row, 34px, whatever the count. Four chips use 659 of the 1,120px available, so they never wrap at this width — and if they ever did, the row grows downwards, away from the controls rather than through them.
Both fields 38px, all four visible, all four removable, and the word Comparing: says what the row is for so the relationship does not rest on position alone.

Aligned exactly under the control that made them — and 659px of chips in a 271px column is three rows, about 100px, which pushes the chart down again. It solves the height and reintroduces the shove.
Recommendation: C. The field carries the count, which is Carbon's answer and the only thing that fits in 256px; the chips go in a labelled row directly beneath the bar, which is Polaris' and Material's. It is the only option where all four are visible and removable and both fields stay 38px.

Why not B: it hides three of four behind a badge, and being able to clear a choice without opening the panel is exactly what was asked for two changes ago.

On proximity: the row sits immediately under the bar and is prefixed Comparing:, so the relationship is carried by a word as well as by position. Tucking the row inside the Compare column instead would align it perfectly and cost three rows of height — which is the problem being fixed.
