Shipping cost — where a conditionally revealed field goes

The Shipping cost field on /distributions/new only applies when the delivery method is Shipped. Right now it is a different width from every other field on the card, and it is drawn above and to the right of the option that produces it. Numbers below are measured from the running app at a 1440px viewport.

What is there nowmeasured

The card's fields sit on a three column grid: 346px each, at x = 309, 675 and 1041. The delivery block opens a second, two column grid, so Shipping cost is 529px — 183px wider than every other field, with a left edge at x = 858 that lands on no column line. Its label sits at y = 503; the Shipped radio that reveals it is at y = 597, 94px below it.

Three separate problems. The width matches nothing; the left edge aligns to nothing; and cause sits below effect, so a keyboard user tabbing off Shipped is thrown 94px back up the page. When the method is Pick up or Delivery — two of the three options, and the default — the right half of the band is simply empty.

Option A — reveal it under the option that triggers itrecommended

The field appears directly beneath “Shipped”, indented and marked with a left rule that runs from the radio it belongs to. This is the GOV.UK / NHS / USWDS conditional reveal, the most heavily tested pattern of the three. The delivery block joins the same three column grid as the rest of the card, so the fieldset is 346px — and the revealed field fills the band inside it. Click the radios; this one works.

  • Cause and effect are adjacent. The field is attached to the one option that needs it, not parked beside the group.
  • Reading order, visual order and tab order finally agree. Today only the first and third do.
  • Nothing reserves empty space. The row does not hold a gap open for a field that is usually absent.
  • The radio can say it expands something — aria-controls and aria-expanded, which it carries neither of now.
  • Cost: the field is 291px, not 346px — the indent takes 55px. The left rule is what makes that read as deliberate.

Option B — keep it beside the group, on the three column gridalternative

The literal reading of “match the three column width”: the delivery block uses the same grid as the rest of the card, the fieldset takes column one and Shipping cost takes column two. Width and alignment are fixed — 346px, landing on x = 675 like everything above it. Position is not.

Ambiguous with three options. Sitting flush under the fieldset, the field reads as belonging to the group — so it looks like it applies to Pick up and Delivery too. The indent and rule in Option A exist precisely to say this belongs to that one option.

What the industry does

SystemPatternWhere the revealed field goes
GOV.UKRadios — “Conditionally revealing a question” Directly below the triggering radio; margin-left 18px, padding-left 33px, 4px left rule. Guidance: only one or two questions per reveal.
NHSRadios with conditional contentSame shape — forked from GOV.UK, same indent and rule.
USWDSRadio buttons — conditional revealBelow the trigger, indented, tied by an indent rule.
CarbonProgressive disclosure guidanceNo named component; guidance is that revealed content follows its trigger in reading order.
Material / Polaris—Nothing formal; both place dependent fields after the control they depend on.

None of the five puts a dependent field in a parallel column. The consistent rule is that a conditionally revealed field follows its trigger in the reading order and is visually attached to it. GOV.UK is the only one that has published usability testing on it, and the indent plus rule is what that testing produced.

One implementation note

GOV.UK renders the conditional content visible and hides it with CSS keyed off a class set by an inline script in <head> — not with JavaScript after paint. That is the same conclusion as the fix landed yesterday: whichever way round, the field must never be painted and then removed. Here the server already knows the delivery method, so it just renders the right state.

Recommendation

Option A. It is the only one of the three that fixes the position as well as the width, it is the pattern three government design systems converged on independently, and it lets the radio announce that it expands something. The 291px field is narrower than a full column, and that is the pattern working as intended — the rule down the left is what tells you why.
