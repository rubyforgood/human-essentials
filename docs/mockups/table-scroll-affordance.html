Nothing says these tables scroll

Measured on /distributions: 486px of columns are off screen, the scrollbar takes 0px of height because it is an overlay one, and there is no fade, shadow or hint of any kind. The only signifier in the markup is aria-label="Table, scrollable", which a sighted mouse user never hears.

Why it went unnoticed

The region was built for the keyboard and the screen reader, and both are served: it is a focusable role="region" with a name, so it is reachable by Tab and announced as scrollable. The audits check exactly that — and an overlay scrollbar is invisible to a computed-style check, so nothing flagged the one group of users left out: people looking at it with a mouse.

Recommendation

D, both — but the fade is the part that has to work. They answer different questions: the fade says there is more that way before you interact with anything, and the scrollbar says how much and can be dragged.

The scrollbar half is platform-dependent and I could not verify it. ::-webkit-scrollbar takes 0px in this headless Chromium even with overlay scrollbars disabled, and Firefox ignores it and offers no way to force a scrollbar to reserve space. So it goes in as an improvement where the platform honours it — not as the answer to "nothing says this scrolls". The fade is the answer, because it works everywhere and depends on nothing.

The fade has to be on a wrapper, not the scroller. A pseudo-element inside the scrolling box scrolls away with the content, which is the usual way this is got wrong.

Directional, not permanent. A fade that is always on both edges is decoration; one that appears only where content is hidden is information. That needs a scroll listener, which is a small Stimulus controller setting data-overflow to start, end, both or neither.

The fade is decoration and must stay that way — pointer-events: none, no text, and the region keeps its existing name. It is a second signal for people who were already served by the first, not a replacement for it.
