Two scrollbars, and one of them is loud

The ghost is not a rendering glitch. The app builds a horizontal scrollbar twice for the same table, and both are meant to be permanently visible.

The bug

Defect

Two separate mechanisms, each solving the same problem, both switched on:

WhereWhat it does
.table-scroll in CSSForces the native scrollbar to be permanently visible. The comment says why: "A scrollbar that is there before you touch it. The platform default on macOS is an overlay that appears mid-gesture."
.table-rail + table_scroll_controllerBuilds a second, custom scrollbar that floats at the bottom of the window, then settles below the table.

Measured, the rail's top edge sits at 637 and the scroll region's bottom edge sits at 637 — so the native bar occupies the last ~10px inside the region and the rail begins immediately below it. Two bars, touching.

That is the ghost. And it moves, which is why it seems to come and go: while the table runs past the fold the rail floats at the bottom of the window, and as the table's end scrolls into view the rail travels down to meet it — passing the native bar on the way.

Today: a native bar, then a 25px rail carrying a 92%-white backdrop over a table row, a top border, a track and a thumb — six elements where a scrollbar needs one or two.

The fix for the ghost, which is not a choice

Hide the native scrollbar wherever the rail exists. The rail is already a complete scrollbar — it drags, it takes a click on the track, it tracks position. Keeping the native one as well is what puts two bars on screen.

It also removes something the CSS comment already admits is unreliable: forcing a persistent native scrollbar "is an improvement where the platform honours it and not the signal itself". Firefox never honours it. The rail behaves the same everywhere.

Worth saying plainly: I could not reproduce your platform's exact rendering here — this container forces overlay scrollbars, so both stylings measure as taking no space. What is not in doubt is that two scrollbars are built for one region; that is in the code, not in the rendering.

Then: how loud should the remaining one be?

The edge fade already carries the signal — the CSS says so: "The fade below is the signal, because it depends on nothing." So the bar does not have to shout that there is more to see. It only has to be a usable control.

  • Two elements instead of six. The 25px bar, the 92% backdrop and the border all go; the track and thumb stay.
  • The click target survives, which is the thing A gives up — and the track is what tells you how much of the table you are seeing.
  • Thumb drops to slate-300, matching every other resting-state line in the app. slate-400 is the app's hover weight, and the bar sits at that weight all the time today.
  • Nothing covers a row. The backdrop existed to keep the bar legible over content; a 6px pill on a faint track does not need one.

Recommendation

Hide the native scrollbar, and restyle the rail as B.

The ghost goes because there is only one scrollbar left. The heaviness goes because the rail stops being a bar with a backdrop and a border, and becomes what it actually is: a track and a thumb. Six elements to two, and the thumb at the app's resting weight rather than its hover weight.
