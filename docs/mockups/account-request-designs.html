Account request page — designs

/account_requests/new sits on the auth shell beside Log in but was built before most of the form rules in design.md. Three options below, all from the app’s own stylesheet. Each frame is the shell’s form column (384px), the width Log in uses. Measured on this branch at 1440×900, 19 October 2026.

What is wrong today, whichever option is chosen

  1. The question is not a question. Two loose radios with no <fieldset> or <legend>, so a screen reader announces two long sentences and never says what they answer. design.md, Building a form page: “A radio or checkbox group is a fieldset with a legend.”
  2. Choosing an answer moves the whole page. The shell centres its column vertically, so revealing the form throws the heading from y=378 to y=76: 302px, at the moment of the click.
  3. Four links look like text. “Sign in to Human Essentials”, both directories and “partner app” have no .link-brand and no underline, so nothing but the cursor says they are links.
  4. The callout repeats the radio you just chose against. “Do you partner with banks? Sign in instead” appears only after you have said you are a bank.
  5. The submit row is a card footer with no card. border-t px-5 py-3 puts a rule across the form and indents Submit 20px off the fields’ edge. Log in uses a full-width primary button.
  6. The form breaks out of the column. 560px against 384px for every other auth screen, via a .account-request rule that only kicks in at 1400px. Below that it is the column width anyway.
  7. A rule is in the label. “Request details (min 50 characters)” belongs in a hint, and a 2-row textarea for 50+ characters makes you scroll inside it.
  8. The heading says “bank account” above a choice that includes “I am a partner”. Seven nested .form-inputs divs that style nothing, and an inline <style> doing the reveal.

A — A radio question, and the answer under it

Recommended

The question becomes a <fieldset> through the :essentials_collection wrapper, with a short label per answer and the long definition as its hint. Choosing partner reveals a callout under that answer through conditional_reveal (marked: false, because a callout carries its own box). Choosing bank reveals the form after the question. Column top-aligned so nothing jumps.

What it costs

Nothing new. Every part already exists: the collection wrapper, conditional_reveal, the callout, .link-brand, the primary button. A small Stimulus controller replaces the inline <style>, per “Stimulus first”. The system spec’s choose('account_bank') keeps working if the ids stay.

Who does this

GOV.UK “Conditionally revealing a question”, USWDS and NHS radios with hints, already cited in design.md. Carbon radio groups with helper text.

What it costs

A component the app does not have, for one page. It needs a design.md entry, focus and checked states, a forced-colours check, and an audit, and every later two-way choice will be argued against it. The tile tint also makes this page the only auth screen with a coloured block before you have done anything.

Who does this

Carbon RadioTile, Cloudscape Tiles, Salesforce Visual Picker. All for a choice that changes what follows, which this is.

C — Ask first, on a page of its own

Changes the flow

The question is the whole first page, with Continue. Bank goes to the form, and partner goes to a short “you don’t need an account” page. Nothing is hidden, so nothing has to be revealed.

What it costs

An extra click and a round trip for every bank, plus a new action or a ?account= step, two new screens for the route sweep, and a rewritten system spec. After a failed submit it must come back to step 2, not step 1. It changes upstream’s flow, not just its look.

Who does this

GOV.UK “One thing per page” and its “question pages” pattern, and USWDS step-by-step forms. GOV.UK’s own advice is to move to a page of its own when a reveal is more than a field or two, and the bank reveal here is six.

Recommendation: A

It fixes all eight defects with parts the system already has, and keeps the flow upstream built. B solves a problem the page does not have: two short answers with hints are already easy to tell apart, and a one-page component is the kind of thing design.md tells you to avoid. C is the better design in theory, since GOV.UK is right that six fields is past what a reveal should hold. But it costs every bank a click to filter out partners, who are the minority here, and it is a product change rather than a design one.

The copy changes in A (a shorter heading and answers, the partner message) apply to B and C too.
