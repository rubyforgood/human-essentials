One row height, or one screen width?

A wide table can have short rows or fit the screen, not both. The demo tables are squeezed to 640px so the effect shows at a comfortable reading width; the figures below them were measured on the real pages at 1440.

Four tables are unaffected — /adjustments, /transfers, /vendors and /product_drives already fit on one line. /item_categories is unaffected too, and that is the interesting one: its 205–439px rows come from a <ul> of item links, which nowrap cannot help. It is the proof that a blanket rule needs an escape hatch.

What the systems do

Carbon, Material, Stripe and Linear all choose the short row and scroll sideways. A table is read down a column — comparing values is the job — and a ragged row height breaks that, while horizontal scroll is a deliberate act you take once. WCAG 1.4.10 Reflow explicitly exempts data tables from the no-horizontal-scroll requirement for the same reason, and this app already puts every table in a focusable .table-scroll region.

The dissent is worth stating: Polaris and GOV.UK prefer fewer columns to more scrolling, on the grounds that a column you have to scroll to is one most people never see. That is an argument for option D below, not against B.

Recommendation

Option C: .data-table td { white-space: nowrap } with a .wrap escape hatch. Cells only — measured, adding th changes nothing, because these columns are sized by their content rather than their headings, and leaving headers free to wrap stops a long future heading widening a column by itself.

The escape hatch is not hypothetical. /item_categories holds a list of links in a cell and has to wrap, as would any cell holding a paragraph rather than a value. Without .wrap the next person meets the rule as an obstacle and reaches for an inline style.

Three tables that fit today would start scrolling — /donations, /storage_locations and /partners, by 72–112px. That is the honest cost, and the reason to say yes deliberately rather than by default: they gain 11–18% in height and lose being readable without moving.

D is the better long answer for /distributions and should follow. Twelve columns is a lot, two of them are adjacent dates, and shipping cost is empty on every row in the seed data. Dropping a column makes the table shorter and narrower, which nowrap cannot. But that is a decision per table about what the page is for; B/C is the rule that applies to all of them.
