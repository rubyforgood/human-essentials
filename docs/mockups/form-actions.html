Where a form's buttons go

Asked of New item, New kit “and so on”: the button sits inside the card with a divider above it, and that does not look right.

“all of those pages have the button nested within the card with a divider separating them. Check the design system for the pattern on this, this does not look right.”

Checked, and the design system has no pattern for this. `design.md` says exactly one thing about a form's action row — that the primary button comes first, because the row is left-aligned — and nothing at all about where the row lives or how it is separated from the fields. So the app has drifted into three different answers.

The 33 are the ones that look wrong, and there is a measurement for it. That divider is drawn inside the card's 20px body padding, so it stops short of both edges: measured on /items/new, 854px of rule in an 896px card — inset 21px on each side, with 21px of card left below it. Every other divider in the app is full bleed: the card header's, the card footer's, the table row dividers, the pagination strip. This is the only rule in the app that does not reach the edges of the thing it is dividing, which is what makes it read as a stray line rather than a boundary.

Measured for comparison on /users/new, which uses the real footer slot: 958px of rule in a 960px card — edge to edge.

A — the card's footer slot

Recommended31 views change

The card component already has a footer: slot, and it draws exactly this: a strip at the foot of the card with a full-bleed rule above it. Two forms already use it, and so does every paginated table, the admin dashboard cards and the transfers page. The buttons stay attached to the surface they act on, and the rule becomes a boundary between two parts of one card rather than a line drawn across the middle of one.

B — keep them in the body, drop the rule

33 views change

The smallest change: delete the border and keep the spacing. The buttons sit under the last field as part of the same block.

The argument against is that the fields and the actions are different kinds of thing, and with nothing between them the Save button reads as one more field in the stack — particularly on the long forms, where the last field is a 3-row textarea and the button lands directly beneath it.

C — below the card entirely

33 views changeone rule for all 42 forms

The buttons leave the card and sit under it, on the page background, with no divider at all. This is already what all seven multi-card forms do — donations, purchases, distributions, transfers, audits, adjustments and New kit — and it is the only treatment the app applies consistently anywhere.

It also has the cleanest argument behind it: the buttons belong to the form, not to a card. A card groups related content; Save submits the whole thing. On New kit that is obvious, because there are three cards and the button belongs to none of them. On New item the distinction is invisible — but the rule that follows from it is the same either way, which is why this is the only option that ends with one rule instead of two.

The argument against is that a button on the page background is less anchored than one on a surface, and on a short form it can look adrift. Against that: the seven forms already doing it are the longest forms in the app, where it reads fine.

What I would ship, and the honest caveat

A, if the complaint is the stray rule — it uses a slot the card already has, makes the divider full bleed like every other divider in the app, and touches 31 views.

C, if the complaint is the nesting — it is the only option that leaves the app with a single rule for all 42 forms rather than one rule for single-card forms and another for multi-card ones, and seven forms already work that way.

Your words were “nested within the card with a divider”, which could be either, so I have not guessed. A keeps the buttons on the surface and fixes the line; C takes them off it and removes the line entirely.

Whichever you pick, design.md gains the rule it never had, and `profiles/edit` and `kits/allocations` — the two views in the 33 that are not single-card forms — get handled explicitly rather than swept along with the rest.
