Four buttons, and one of them is in the wrong place

/requests is the only page in the app that breaks this rule — the audit checked 27 page headers across three roles and found one.

The rule already exists, and it matches the industry

design.md: “At most three actions, exactly one of them primary, primary last. Past three, the least-used collapse behind a ‘More actions’ menu. A page has one place for its main action. If a fourth button appears, that is the signal that something else is wrong — usually a section of the page wanting an action of its own.”

That is the industry position too. Shopify Polaris, Atlassian, Salesforce Lightning and GitHub Primer all give a page one primary action and overflow the rest into a menu after two or three. The reason is not tidiness: a page with four equal-looking buttons has no main action, because everything emphasised is nothing emphasised.

The rule was written and never enforced. design.md even anticipated the check — “the actions container carries `data-page-header="actions"` so a spec can count what is in it” — and nothing ever counted. That is why a fourth button could arrive unnoticed. bin/design/button-audit.js counts now.

What “Calculate product totals” actually does

It opens a modal listing the total quantity requested for each item, across every request matching the current filters. It works, and it does follow the filters — narrowing to one partner took it from 46 items and 23,035 units to a smaller set, and I confirmed that against the row count. So the calculation is real.

Three things are wrong with it, and none of them is the maths.

  1. There is no total. The modal lists 46 per-item figures and never adds them up. The button promises a total and the panel gives a breakdown — the sum, 23,035 units, is the one number it does not show.
  2. The verb is wrong. design.md: buttons take a verb, and the verb is what will happen. Nothing is calculated when you press it; the figures already exist. It shows them.
  3. It is in the wrong place. It is not an action on the page, it is a summary of the table below it — which is exactly the “section of the page wanting an action of its own” the rule predicts when a fourth button turns up.

  • For: two buttons, one row at every width, and the primary is unmistakable.
  • For: it groups by kind rather than by how much room is left — outputs together, the summary with its table, the main action alone.
  • Against: the picklist goes from one click to two. It is the least-used of the four, and it is still one press from the page.
  • Against: a menu needs the popover controller and its keyboard handling — already built for row actions, so this is reuse rather than new work.

C — A “More actions” menu, as the rule literally says

Collapse the two least-used behind one trigger and change nothing else.

  • For: exactly what design.md prescribes, so no new argument is needed.
  • Against: “More actions” names nothing. A menu called Export tells you what is inside it; this one tells you there is something inside it.
  • Against: it leaves the totals summary in the page header, which is the actual mistake. It fixes the count and not the cause.

And the modal itself

Give it the total. A <tfoot> row with the sum — 23,035 units across 46 items on the unfiltered set. That is the number the button's name promises.

There is a precedent against <tfoot> totals here, from the reports work: they were dropped on /distributions because a summary band above the table was already saying the same thing, and “the band and the footer saying overlapping things is worse than either alone”. Nothing duplicates this one — the modal is the only place these figures appear — so the argument does not carry across.

And rename it “Show product totals”, since nothing is calculated on press and the convention is that a button's verb is what will happen.
