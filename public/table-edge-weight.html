You are right, and the reason is measurable: what is shipped drops the row background from 250 to 191 at the table's edge, tinting 28.5% of the table you can see. That is six times Ant Design's value for the same signal, and heavier than anything current ships.

Why I picked something that heavy

A bad metric. To decide whether an edge signal was visible at all, I averaged the luminance change over a 68px strip. That measure rewards a broad smear and punishes a crisp line: a 1px rule changes almost no area, so it scored 1.83 and I wrote it off as imperceptible. It is perfectly visible — the eye finds edges by local contrast, not by area average.

Measuring the same candidates by peak drop and sharpest local step instead:

Recommendation

B — Ant Design's value, 6% over 10px.

It is the reference value for exactly this signal in the table component more people use than any other, it is a sixth of what is shipped, and it stops the edge reading as a vignette while still marking the boundary. The rail does the work of saying you can scroll; this only has to say the content is cut here rather than ending here.

A is the other defensible answer and I would not argue hard against it: with a rail present the edge shading is arguably redundant, and “nothing” can never look dated. The case for keeping something is that the rail sits at the bottom of the table while your eye is in the middle of it.

C is a trap. It measures best on sharpness, but a 1px vertical line at the edge of a card that already has a 1px border reads as the edge of the card — it says “this is the boundary”, which is the opposite of “there is more past here”. Right numbers, wrong meaning.

D is a compromise with nothing to recommend it over B beyond being closer to what is already there.

One more to decide while we are here

The frozen column casts its own shadow when content passes under it, currently 28% of slate-900 — also far above Ant Design's 6% for the same thing. It is a tighter shadow than the edge one, so it reads less heavily, but if the edge drops to 6% this should come with it. Ant uses the same value for both, and one number for one meaning is the better rule.
