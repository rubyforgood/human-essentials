The Service area card, again

You are right that it is hard to read. The card exists so a bank can compare what a partner says it covers with where its families actually are — and it puts two facts near each other and leaves you to do the comparing.

  • The right column is 273px tall and holds one number — measured, about 85% empty, next to a four-row table.
  • Two headings and two captions — four lines of chrome to carry two facts.
  • "See zipcodes" is in the card header, away from the 13 it belongs to. That is the same disconnection this card was built to fix, at smaller scale.
  • They are not parallel things. One is a table of counties with shares; one is a single count. Forcing them into equal columns was the mistake.

  • It makes the comparison — in a sentence, which is the card's whole reason for existing.
  • Stacked, not columned. A table and a list are different shapes; full width lets long county names sit on one line and sorts by share so the biggest is first.
  • The zipcodes are shown, not hidden. Measured across this bank: max 13, median 8 per partner — the list fits. Past a dozen it truncates with "and 7 more", which opens the dialog; below that the dialog never appears.
  • Against: the card gets taller, and "13" stops being a big number you can spot from across the page.

Recommendation

A. The card was added to make one comparison possible, and A is the only option that actually makes it rather than arranging the parts and hoping. Stacking also admits what the two-column layout denied: a table of counties and a list of zipcodes are not parallel things and were never going to balance.

If you would rather keep the numbers big and glanceable, B is the one — it trades the sentence for two figures and keeps the dialog.
