Two problems, and the first one is mine

The Week button really is broken, on a narrower window — and the reason is a decision I made two turns ago. Reaching March next year currently takes seven clicks.

1. Why Week does nothing

Window widthView on loadClicking "Week"
1440, 1200, 1000month gridweek grid
991 and belowa list, with Week already pressednothing at all

I mapped one label to two different views: "Week" means a week grid above 992px and a list below it. And below 992 the list is also the default — so Week is already the current view, the switch returns early, and the button is inert. Month works, so only Week looks broken.

992px is not an unusual width: a 1440 screen at 150% scaling is 960 CSS px, and any window that is not maximised can land under it.

The mistake was the mapping, not the threshold. A label should mean one thing. The list is a different view, not a narrow rendering of a week.

Now, below 992px

Week is pressed on arrival, shows a list, and clicking it does nothing.

Proposed

Three views, each meaning one thing. List stays the default on a narrow window; Week always gives the week grid.

2. Getting to a month that is not next month

Prev and Next move one step. To reach March 2027 from today is seven clicks; to check last December, five. There is no other way.

What other calendars do

ProductHow you jump
Google CalendarA mini month in the sidebar, always visible; click any date. Plus a year view.
OutlookThe same: a persistent mini calendar for navigation.
Apple CalendarA Year view — click a month to enter it.
Notion, LinearClick the month title; a small month/year picker opens.
Stripe, Metabase and other data toolsNot month navigation at all — a date range filter over a list.

The split at the bottom of that table matters. A personal calendar navigates; a data tool filters. This page is a schedule of distributions, which puts it somewhere between the two — but the app has already chosen a side elsewhere, and it has a component for it.

The options

A — A month and year picker beside the title recommended

Two native <select>s. Any month of any year in two clicks, from a control the browser already knows how to open, and no dependency.

  • For: it is the app's own precedent. Litepicker was deleted in favour of native inputs — "No calendar widget and no third-party dependency" — and this is the same argument.
  • For: a <select> works in every browser, on every device, with a keyboard, and needs nothing written for it.
  • For: the year list can be bounded by the data — there is no point offering 1990.
  • Against: two controls where one would do, and it takes horizontal room in a toolbar that already has five things.
  • Against: it replaces the title, so the heading has to move or go.

B — A native month input

One control: <input type="month">.

Live above — how it looks depends entirely on your browser.

  • For: one control, one concept, and the same native argument as A.
  • Against, and I could not test it here: only Chromium is installed in this environment, so I have no measurement for Firefox or Safari. By documentation, type="month" is well supported in Chrome and Edge and not in desktop Firefox or Safari, where it degrades to a plain text box expecting "2026-08". That is a worse control than a select, silently, for some of your users.
  • Given that, A is the same idea without the gamble.

C — A clickable title with a month grid

Click "August 2026", get a small panel of months and a year stepper. What Notion and Linear do.

  • For: no extra chrome in the toolbar; the title earns its place.
  • Against: a heading that is secretly a button is a thing you have to discover, and this app's own rule is that an icon-only or unlabelled control needs its name said out loud.
  • Against: it is a popover to build, keyboard-handle and test — the work A avoids by using a control that already exists.

D — A date range filter, like the other index pages

The app already has shared/date_range_picker: presets on one side, two native date fields on the other. Every other index page has it. This page has none.

  • For: it answers the second half of your question literally — a range, not a month.
  • Against: a calendar grid shows a month or a week. Handing it "3 March to 19 August" gives it nothing to draw; it would have to change what the page is.
  • Where a range does belong: /distributions, which is the same data as a list and already has that filter. The calendar answers "what is happening around now"; the index answers "what happened between these dates".

Recommendation

Fix the views to Month / Week / List, and add A, the month and year selects.

The view fix is not optional — a button that does nothing is a defect, and it is one I introduced by giving "Week" two meanings.

A for the jump, because the app already decided this question when it deleted Litepicker: native controls over a widget. Two selects reach any month of any year, work everywhere, and cost nothing to maintain. B is the same idea in one control and I cannot promise it renders as a picker for everyone, which is exactly the kind of claim this branch has been caught making before.

Not D, here. A range is a real need and this is the wrong page for it: the grid cannot draw an arbitrary span. /distributions already answers it, and if that is what you are reaching for, the honest fix is a link from this page to that one rather than a filter that the calendar cannot honour.
