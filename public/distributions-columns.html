Fewer columns on /distributions

Twelve columns in a 1,118px region. Every figure below was measured on the page, and two assumptions I had written down earlier turned out to be wrong.

Two things I had recorded that were not true

“Shipping cost is empty on every row.” It is present on 50% of them. I had looked at one visible row and generalised.

“Two of them are adjacent dates”, implying redundancy. The created date and the issued date differ on all 24 rows. They are two different facts and dropping one loses information.

The test is whether the value is one click away. Delivery method, shipping cost and comments are all on the detail page, and none of them is a filter axis — so dropping them costs a click, not the data. Initial allocation is the opposite: it appears nowhere else in the app, so it can only go after it is added to the detail page.

Dropping columns alone never makes it fit — and the biggest single win is not a data column.

Actions is 331px, the second-widest column in the table, holding up to five labelled buttons. One action plus an overflow menu takes it to about 80px: a 251px saving that costs no data at all. Dropping the three safe data columns saves 355px. Both together land at 1,153px — 35px over, which is a scrollbar nobody will notice. Fitting exactly needs a fourth column gone.

The cons of removing columns

  • Scanning across rows is what a table is for. A column lets you compare fourteen rows at a glance; the detail page shows one. “Which of these went by courier?” goes from one look to fourteen page loads. This is the whole cost and everything else is a detail of it.
  • Ctrl-F stops finding it. People search the page for a value far more than we expect. A dropped column is invisible to that, silently.
  • The export and the table start disagreeing. The CSV keeps its columns. Once the table has fewer, two views of the same records differ, and people stop trusting whichever is smaller.
  • It is a bet placed with the wrong evidence. We have seed data, not usage data. Shipping cost is 50% empty here; at a bank that ships everything it is 100% full and the most-read column on the page.
  • Nobody files a complaint about a missing column. They just do the job more slowly, or export to a spreadsheet and do it there. The cost is real and silent, which is the worst combination for a decision you want to revisit.
  • This page is a reconciliation surface. People tally money off it. Removing a money column — shipping cost, total value — is a different order of risk from removing delivery method.

Recommendation

1. Shrink Actions first, and possibly only that. 251px for no data loss is the best trade on the page, and it is the change with no downside to weigh: View stays visible and the rest — View request, Print, Edit, Reclaim — go behind a menu. It also fixes the same problem on /items, /vendors and /requests, which carry three to five actions each. That needs its own preview, because an overflow menu is a component this app does not have.

2. Then drop Delivery method and Comments. Both on the detail page, neither a filter axis, and comments is already clipped to one line so the column shows almost nothing. 247px.

3. Keep Shipping cost, against my own arithmetic. It is the third “safe” column by the one-click test and I would still keep it, because it is money on a page people reconcile from, and because 50%-empty in seed data tells us nothing about a bank that ships everything. If it goes, it should go after asking a bank, not after measuring a fixture.

Not recommended: dropping Initial allocation to hit 1,118px exactly. Chasing the round number would remove the only place a distribution's created date appears. Adding it to the detail page first would make it droppable — but a 35px overflow is not worth a migration of information.
