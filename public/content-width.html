Where the page ends

Centring the card

Fixes one page, leaves the rest

  • The heading moves with it. Every h1 in this app sits at 288 — the blue line above. Centring the form pushes its title 144–256px right, so the page title slides sideways as you go from the items list to the item form.
  • It does nothing for the tables. Distributions still renders at 2238 for content that needs 1505. The form stops looking stranded; the actual waste is untouched.
  • It treats the symptom on one page. The cause is that the page has no end.

Capping the container

Recommended

One class on the layout — mx-auto w-full max-w-[100rem] — so the whole page has an end, and centres once the monitor is wider than it.

  • Nothing changes below 1600. At 1440 the content area is 1184 — already under the cap — so a laptop sees exactly what it sees today. The change only appears where the problem does.
  • The tables come back to a readable width. 1600 leaves 1536 inside the gutters, and the widest table in the app needs 1505. Every one fits without scrolling.
  • No heading jump, because the cap applies to every page equally. An index page and its form both sit in the same container, so the h1 stays put as you move between them — which is the thing centring the card would have broken.
  • The card stays left inside the container. Its remaining space is bounded and the page around it is balanced, instead of a void hanging off one side that grows without limit.
  • Against: anyone who deliberately maximises a wide window to see more table at once will now get letterboxing instead. That is the trade — and at 2560 they were getting 733px of stretched whitespace inside the table, not more information.

Recommendation

Cap the container at 1600 and centre it. Leave the card left-aligned inside it.

You were right that something is wrong with the space — but the card was the place it happened to show. Centring the card would have made one form look composed while every table in the app kept stretching to 3120px on a wide monitor. Capping the page fixes both, and keeps the card's alignment correct.

1600 is chosen from the data, not the eye: the widest table needs 1505, so it is the smallest round cap that never introduces a horizontal scroll.
