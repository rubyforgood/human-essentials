A week earns its place. A day does not.

There is no view switching on this page at all — FullCalendar's default toolbar has none unless you ask, and this app never did. Below 992px it swaps to a week list, which is the only view choice being made and it is made for you.

What the data says about each view

Over the last year
Days with any distribution22 — mean 1.9, max 10
Days holding exactly one13 of 22
Weeks with any12 — mean 3.5, max 16

A day view would be an hour axis for 1.9 events, and on 13 of 22 days for a single one. Twenty-four rows of chrome to say "Silver Spring, 9am" — which the month cell already says in one line. That is the clearest answer here: Day does not earn it.

A week averages 3.5 and peaks at 16. Sixteen in a week is the case the month grid handles worst — that is where "+5 more" appears and you lose the detail. A week is also the horizon the page is for: what is going out, and what has to be packed before it.

One thing that decides which week

The times are real. The distribution form takes as: :datetime, minute_step: 15, labelled "Distribution date and time". So a bank genuinely records that a pick-up is at 9:00.

But a distribution has no end. The columns are created_at, updated_at, issued_at — there is no duration to draw. On a time grid every event is a zero-length block.

And half the rows in a fresh database have no time at all. 24 of 48 sit at 00:00, because db/seeds.rb sets a date and no time. On a time grid those stack at the very top of every day, which reads as a bug rather than as missing data. (The 24 with real hours are the ones db:seed:calendar made.)

The options

A — Month and Week, as day cells recommended

dayGridWeek: the same cells as the month, seven of them, each with room for every event instead of "+5 more". Times are shown as text on the chip, as they already are in the month.

The six-event Friday, whole. In the month grid that same day is one chip and "+5 more".

  • For: solves the case the month is worst at, which is the busy day.
  • For: no new plugin — dayGrid is already loaded, so no importmap pin and nothing new to keep on a version.
  • For: nothing it draws is a promise the data cannot keep — no axis implying durations that do not exist, and a 00:00 row just reads as a chip with a time you can ignore.
  • Against: it does not show the shape of a day. Three pick-ups at 9am and three spread across it look identical.

Note the 00:00 row, and how much of the grid is empty at a mean of 3.5 events per week. The axis is truncated here; the real one runs to 24 rows.

  • For: shows the shape of a day, which is the thing a warehouse actually schedules against.
  • For: it is what "week view" means to anyone who has used a calendar.
  • Against: a new plugin and a new importmap pin, on a library the app has already been caught trailing a major version of.
  • Against: zero-length events on a time axis, because a distribution has no end. Every block is a hairline.
  • Against, and it is the one that decides it: the 00:00 rows. Half of a fresh database piles at midnight, and a grid that puts them there is showing missing data as if it were a 12am appointment.

C — Month, Week and Day, the full set

  • For: the conventional set, and nobody has to ask where Day went.
  • Against: 1.9 events a day, and one on 13 days out of 22. A whole view for a line and a half.
  • Against: a third control to fit on a phone, where the page is already a list.

Does the switcher remember?

Three ways, and the app has already answered this once.

Where the choice lives
Nowhere — always MonthSimplest. Costs a click every visit for anyone who lives in the week view.
localStorageWhat Google and Outlook do. Invisible: two people looking at "the calendar" see different pages, and neither can send the other what they are looking at.
The URL — ?view=week recommendedLinkable, bookmarkable, survives Back, and shareable in a message to a colleague.

The URL, because design.md already made this call. On page tabs: "it is also how a tab becomes something you can link to, bookmark and go back from." The same argument applies without change — a view is a tab by another name.

It is also the only one of the three that answers "why is mine different from yours".

And it costs nothing to also remember. Landing on /distributions/schedule with no parameter can use your last choice, while any URL that names a view wins. That gives the convenience without making the page unshareable — but it is a second mechanism, so only worth it if the click actually annoys you.

Recommendation

A, with the view in the URL. No Day view.

Week as day cells, because it fixes the month grid's worst case — the crowded day — without drawing an hour axis this data cannot honestly fill. If banks start recording times on everything and the midnight rows disappear, B becomes the better answer and the switch is a one-line change to the view name.

Day is rejected on the numbers, not on taste: 1.9 events a day, one on 13 days out of 22.
